How it works.

A

Track.

Ships broadcast their position constantly, every few minutes, all day, on a public radio channel. Point a feed at a stretch of water and the traffic simply arrives.

B

Display.

Run Portsviews on a Raspberry Pi, a laptop, an old tablet — anything that can drive a screen. Those same ships appear, exactly where they really are, moving in real time.

C

Leave it on.

No app to open, no login to check. Once it's running, it just keeps going.

What you get.

  • Live ship positions and headings for the Dover StraitEvery vessel drawn where it actually is, pointing the way it is actually going.
  • Ship type, name, speed and a running countThe largest moving vessels are named on screen; the rest are drawn by type.
  • The real coastline under the trafficKent and the Pas-de-Calais, drawn from OpenStreetMap data, not a decorative backdrop.

Why it's free.

Ship data is cheap to receive and expensive to resell. A licensed commercial feed for a single busy strait runs to hundreds of dollars a month before a single customer exists, and every free source worth using says plainly that it is not for resale. So a paid subscription would mean either charging enough to cover a bill that scales with every view we add, or quietly breaking somebody's terms.

The alternative is better anyway. Ship positions are broadcast in the open, and anyone within range of a coastline can receive them with a thirty-dollar radio dongle. There is no reason that should be a subscription. So the renderer is open source, the whole thing is free to run, and what you point it at is your business.

A box that just works.

Building it yourself means a few evenings of setup. Not everyone wants that evening. So we are putting together a kit: a small computer, a radio receiver and antenna, and the software already installed and pointed at your coast. Plug it in, point it at a wall, and it runs.

It does not exist yet. There is no price, no date, and nothing to buy on this page. When it is real, it will be funded the way hardware like this should be — publicly, up front, with the numbers shown. The waitlist below is how you hear about that first.

Frequently Asked Questions.

Is it really free?

Yes. The software is open source and there is nothing to buy, subscribe to, or sign in for. The kit, when it exists, will cost money — that is the only part that ever will.

What do I need to run it myself?

A computer that can run Node, a screen or projector, and a source of ship positions. A free online feed works for trying it; a small radio receiver works better and keeps everything local.

What is the kit?

A ready-made version of exactly that: the computer, the receiver, the antenna, and the software set up for where you live. It is not built yet, and there is nothing to pay today.

When is the kit coming?

We are not going to guess a date. It will be crowdfunded, which means the date becomes real when the campaign does, and you will hear about it from this list before anywhere else.

Which coastlines does it work on?

Anywhere ships broadcast, which is most busy water. The demo above is the Dover Strait because that is what we recorded. What you point yours at is up to you.

What do you do with my email?

We store your address so we can tell you when the kit is real. Nothing else — no name, no location, no tracking. More in the privacy policy.
